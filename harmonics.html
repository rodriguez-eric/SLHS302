Periodic and aperiodic sounds

A pure tone has a single frequency. A complex sound contains multiple frequency components. A periodic complex wave repeats its entire shape at regular intervals. An aperiodic waveform, such as a short noise example, has no fixed repeat period.

Build a repeating wave

The fundamental f₀ is the repeat rate of the whole waveform, or 1/T. A harmonic is a whole-number multiple of f₀. Adjust the amplitude or phase of the 200 Hz component while all three tones are on. The shape changes while the 10 ms period stays fixed.

Try it 1 · Match frequencies to harmonic numbers

For f₀ = 25 Hz the first four harmonic frequencies are 25, 50, 75, and 100 Hz. A wave with only 50, 75, and 100 Hz still has a 25 Hz fundamental. Its period is 40 ms, even with the first harmonic absent.

Octaves and harmonics

An octave doubles frequency. Going up whole octaves from f₀ reaches the 2nd, 4th, 8th, and 16th harmonics. These harmonic numbers are powers of two. An octave can start from another harmonic too. For example, the 3rd and 6th harmonics are one octave apart.
