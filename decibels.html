Pressure, Intensity, and Decibels

What are we measuring?

Sound pressure is pressure variation around ambient pressure. Use RMS pressure in Pa for sound pressure level. Sound intensity is power per unit area in W/m². Decibels describe a logarithmic ratio. A measurement equal to its reference is 0 dB.

Why 10 for intensity and 20 for pressure?

One bel equals 10 decibels, giving 10 log₁₀(I₂/I₁). For comparable propagation conditions, intensity is proportional to RMS pressure squared. Substitute the squared pressure ratio and apply the log power rule.

10 log₁₀(I₂/I₁) = 10 log₁₀[(p₂/p₁)²] = 20 log₁₀(p₂/p₁).

Worked examples and practice

Pressure → level. 0.01 Pa ÷ 0.00002 Pa = 500. Take LOG(500) ≈ 2.698970, then multiply by 20 to get 53.98 dB SPL.

Level → pressure. 60 ÷ 20 = 3. Undo LOG with 10³ = 1,000. Multiply 1,000 × 0.00002 Pa to get 0.02 Pa RMS.

Intensity → level. Divide 9 × 10⁻³ by 10⁻¹² to get 9 × 10⁹. Take LOG to get about 9.954243, then multiply by 10 to get 99.54 dB IL.

Level → intensity. 60 ÷ 10 = 6. Undo LOG with 10⁶, then multiply by the reference. 10⁶ × 10⁻¹² = 10⁻⁶ W/m². See Base-10 Logarithms for a refresher.
