Standing Waves and Resonance

What is a standing wave?

A standing wave is an oscillating pattern whose nodes and antinodes stay in the same places. It can form when equal waves travel in opposite directions and overlap. A node stays at equilibrium. An antinode has the greatest amplitude of oscillation.

What is resonance?

Push a swing at the right moment and each push adds energy. A tube responds strongly when driven near a natural frequency. Its length, boundary conditions, and wave speed determine the natural frequencies. Each standing-wave pattern is a mode.

Worked example · Ear canal resonance

Model the ear canal as open at the entrance and closed at the eardrum. The first pressure mode has a node at the entrance and an antinode at the eardrum. For a length of 2.5 cm, λ = 4 × 0.025 = 0.100 m. Then f = 350/0.100 = 3,500 Hz. A longer tube has a lower first resonant frequency.
