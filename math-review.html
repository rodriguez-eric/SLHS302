Right Triangles and the Pythagorean Theorem

First find the little square at the 90° angle. The side across from it is the hypotenuse. From your chosen acute angle, the opposite leg is across from you and the adjacent leg touches your angle. Switch acute angles and the legs trade labels.

Use a² + b² = c². Find the hypotenuse by adding the squared legs and taking the square root. Find either leg by subtracting the known leg’s square from the hypotenuse’s square and taking the square root.

FindGivenCalculation
Adjacent legOpposite 9, hypotenuse 15√(15² − 9²) = 12
Opposite legAdjacent 24, hypotenuse 25√(25² − 24²) = 7
HypotenuseLegs 6 and 8√(6² + 8²) = 10

Degrees, Radians, and Cycles

Degrees divide a turn into 360 parts. Radians measure arc length in units of the radius. Cycles count whole turns and fractions of a turn. 360° = 2π radians = 1 cycle. Divide degrees by 360 or radians by 2π to get cycles. Multiply cycles by 360 or 2π to convert back.

GivenOther measurements
90°90 ÷ 360 = ¼ cycle. ¼ × 2π = π/2 radians.
3π/2 radians(3π/2) ÷ 2π = ¾ cycle. ¾ × 360 = 270°.
½ cycle½ × 360 = 180°. ½ × 2π = π radians.
⅛ cycle⅛ × 360 = 45°. ⅛ × 2π = π/4 radians.

Laws of Exponents

In 10³, 10 is the base and 3 is the exponent. The laws of exponents explain how to simplify expressions with powers. Determine the operation first.

LawRuleWorked example
Product of powersSame base, add exponents10³ × 10² = 10⁵
Quotient of powersSame base, subtract the denominator exponent10² ÷ 10⁵ = 10⁻³
Power of a powerMultiply the exponents(10²)³ = 10⁶
Power of a productApply the exponent to each factor(2 × 10)³ = 2³ × 10³ = 8,000
Power of a quotientApply it to numerator and denominator(2/10)² = 2²/10² = 0.04
Zero exponentAny nonzero base to the zero power gives 110³ ÷ 10³ = 10⁰ = 1
Negative exponentTake the reciprocal10⁻³ = 1/1,000 = 0.001

Scientific Notation

Write a positive number as a coefficient from 1 up to 10, multiplied by a power of 10. The units stay the same. For standard notation → scientific notation, shift the decimal until one nonzero digit is left of it. Moving left gives a positive exponent and moving right gives a negative one. Reverse those shifts to convert back.

1,800,000 = 1.8 × 10⁶. The decimal moves six places left to make 1.8. For 0.0005, move four places right to make 5, so the result is 5 × 10⁻⁴. To convert 4.3 × 10⁻³ back, shift three places left to get 0.0043. Type 5e-4 for 5 × 10⁻⁴.

Base-10 Logarithms

A base-10 logarithm asks which exponent on 10 gives the input. The input must be positive. A number below 1 gives a negative answer, 1 gives zero, and a number above 1 gives a positive answer.

log(10,000) = 4 because 10⁴ = 10,000. log(0.001) = −3 because 10⁻³ = 0.001. Use the LOG key for log(20) ≈ 1.301.

LawRuleExample
Productlog(xy) = log(x) + log(y)log(2 × 10) ≈ 0.301 + 1
Quotientlog(x/y) = log(x) − log(y)log(100/1,000) = 2 − 3
Powerlog(xⁿ) = n log(x)log(100²) = 2 × 2
