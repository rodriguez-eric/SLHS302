How does sound travel?

A vibrating source creates compressions and rarefactions that travel through the medium. Particles oscillate around their own equilibrium positions. Follow the highlighted particle and the marker at its position on the pressure graph.

Period and wavelength

A microphone records pressure over time. A snapshot shows pressure across space. Measure between two consecutive peaks and check the horizontal axis.

Calculate wavelength, frequency, or period

Use 350 m/s for air in these lab problems. Use λ = c/f or λ = cT, f = c/λ, and T = λ/c. Convert kHz to Hz, cm or mm to meters, and ms to seconds.

At 2 kHz, λ = 350/2,000 = 0.175 m. For λ = 20 cm, f = 350/0.20 = 1,750 Hz. For T = 5 ms, λ = 350 × 0.005 = 1.75 m. At fixed speed, higher frequency means shorter wavelength. When a sound enters another medium its frequency stays the same, so a greater speed means a longer wavelength.
