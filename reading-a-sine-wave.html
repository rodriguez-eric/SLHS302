Cycles, Period, and Frequency

A cycle is one complete repetition. The period T is the time for one complete cycle. Frequency f is the number of cycles per second, measured in hertz (Hz). f = 1/T when T is in seconds.

Compare two consecutive peaks or two zero crossings in the same direction. At 1 and 5 ms, consecutive peaks are 4 ms apart. Convert to 0.004 s, then take the reciprocal to get 250 Hz.

Amplitude and RMS

Read instantaneous amplitude at one time and keep its sign. Peak is the distance from equilibrium to the crest. Peak-to-peak is the crest minus the trough. For a sine wave centered at zero, calculate RMS by multiplying peak by 0.707 or dividing it by √2.

RMS stands for root mean square. Square the values, take their mean, then take the square root. For a wave with a +4 Pa crest and −4 Pa trough, the peak is 4 Pa, the span is 8 Pa, and RMS is about 2.828 Pa.

Starting and Instantaneous Phase

Phase describes position within the cycle. Starting phase is the phase at time zero. Instantaneous phase is the phase at a selected time. The vertical coordinate of a point on the unit circle traces the sine wave as the point moves counterclockwise.

At 0° or 360° the wave crosses equilibrium upward. At 90° it is at the crest, at 180° it crosses downward, and at 270° it is at the trough.

Phase at t = starting phase + 360° × (t ÷ T). Use the same units for t and T. For within-cycle phase, subtract full turns. A wave starting at 90° with T = 8 ms advances another 270° by 6 ms, giving 360° total or 0° within one cycle.

Read a Complete Sine Wave

Start with the time axis, then read the pressure axis, then find phase. A wave with peaks of +4 Pa at 0 and 8 ms has a period of 8 ms and frequency of 125 Hz. At 6 ms its pressure is 0 Pa. Its peak is 4 Pa, peak-to-peak is 8 Pa, and RMS is 2.828 Pa. It starts at 90° and reaches 360° total phase at 6 ms.

On a time graph, two consecutive peaks are separated by one period. On a position graph, they are separated by one wavelength.
