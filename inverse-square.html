Distance and the Inverse-Square Law

Why does intensity decrease with distance?

For an ideal source radiating equally in all directions, power spreads across a sphere of area 4πr². Doubling distance quadruples area and reduces intensity to one-quarter.

I₂ = I₁(r₁/r₂)² and level change = 20 log₁₀(r₁/r₂). Add the change to the starting level. A negative change means the sound level drops.

Worked examples and practice

From intensity. At 2 m the intensity is 0.008 W/m². At 4 m the distance ratio is 2/4 = 0.5. Square it to get 0.25. Multiply 0.008 × 0.25 to get 0.002 W/m².

From level. Start at 100 dB SPL at 1 m and move to 3 m. The intensity ratio is (1/3)² = 1/9. Then 10 log₁₀(1/9) ≈ −9.54 dB. The new level is 90.46 dB SPL.

Enter intensity as a decimal or in e notation. For example, 0.00025 W/m² = 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ W/m². Type 0.00025 or 2.5e-4. Round dB to two decimals. For an exact doubling or halving of distance, the ±6 dB shortcut also works.
